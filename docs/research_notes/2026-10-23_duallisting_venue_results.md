# Dual-listed stocks: which line to trade, TSX or NYSE/Nasdaq

Cost parameters (commission, spreads, FX tiers): see [`docs/cost_assumptions.md`](../cost_assumptions.md).

Data fetched 2026-10-23 (yfinance Ticker.history (auto_adjust=False, interval 1d); BoC Valet FXUSDCAD). Generated by `scripts/cost_edge_duallisting.py` from the local snapshot in `research/cost_edge_duallisting/`. FX cost functions, spread estimators and the DLR / DLR-U.TO half-spreads are imported from `scripts/cost_edge_norbert.py` and the Norbert snapshot (`snapshot_2026-10-21`; see `docs/research_notes/2026-10-21_norbert_gambit_results.md`). The broker's tiers, the C$9.99 / US$9.99 commission and the 1-cent tick are input constants read from the broker's published fee pages on 2026-10-18, not re-verified here. All spreads are **estimates** (Corwin-Schultz, Abdi-Ranaldo), not quotes.

## 1. Short answer

- **Holding CAD:** the TSX line is the cheaper venue at 4 of 4 sizes (median name). Buying the US line with CAD means paying the desk's FX spread (or Norbert's gambit) twice; the cheapest FX leg costs 11.0 to 34.0 bp of V per conversion (cheaper of FX desk and gambit, C$10k to C$250k), against a TSX-minus-US half-spread difference of -0.6 bp (median name; largest over all names 9.5 bp).
- **Holding USD:** the US line is the cheaper venue at 4 of 4 sizes; buying the TSX line from USD cash needs the same two conversions. The spread estimates are inflated in absolute terms (section 3), but the verdict depends on the line-to-line difference and on FX, not on the absolute level.
- The gambit cost uses d = 0 and upward-biased half-spread estimates, so its net bias direction is unknown (sections 4 and 8).
- Details, per-size numbers and the exceptions are below; the verdict table is section 6.

## 3. Half-spreads (ESTIMATES) and ADV

Half-spread as a fraction of price. Per line: **used = max(CS, AR, half-tick)** from `cost_edge_norbert.spread_table` (Corwin-Schultz 2012 two-day windows, negatives clamped to 0; Abdi-Ranaldo 2017 monthly, months with at least 8 sessions; the half-tick floor is one step beyond max(CS, AR) and is labelled as such). High-low estimators overstate the spread of mega-caps whose true spread is one tick, so differences of 1-2 bp between lines are inside estimator noise.

Over the 40 names (last 252 sessions): TSX line median half-spread 24.9 bp (p10 16.0, p90 39.8); US line 25.1 bp (p10 17.6, p90 43.8). The TSX half-spread is narrower than the US one for 27 of 40 names (wider for 13, narrower for 27; two-sided exact sign test p = 3.8e-02). Median (TSX - US) = -0.62 bp; share of names where the two lines are within 1 bp of each other: 42%. **The absolute levels are far above what these mega-caps quote** (a high-low estimator on daily bars picks up intraday volatility; typical quoted spreads of the large banks are a fraction of that, UNVERIFIED), so read them as upper bounds. That direction is the majority one but not universal (27 of 40), and the difference is small against the level and inside the 1-2 bp noise of the estimator, so it is a tilt, not a measurement of the true spread gap. See section 6 for whether it decides a verdict.

**The sign test is fragile; the direction is more robust.** p = 0.038 on 13 vs 27 is only just below 5%: moving one name to the other side gives p = 0.081; counting only names whose half-spreads differ by at least 1 bp, TSX wider for 6 and narrower for 17 (p = 0.035); at least 2 bp, 5 vs 11 (p = 0.21). Direction (TSX wider / narrower) under other choices: common-calendar estimates 10 / 30; all 57 pairs that pass the filters 16 / 41; the 40 with the largest US-line ADV 12 / 28; without the 3 pairs whose mapping was corrected 10 / 27. Consolidated US high-low versus TSX-only high-low (venue coverage) is an unresolved confound.

Calendar check: estimating each line on its own exchange calendar (this version) rather than on the TSX-and-US intersection (previous version) changed the TSX half-spread by +0.02 bp on average (largest |change| 3.0 bp) and the US one by -0.14 bp (largest 6.7 bp); the biggest single move is TECK-B.TO|TECK. ## 4. Cost of a round-trip position

Routes (all round trip, C$): a = TSX line from CAD; b = US line from USD already held; c = US line from CAD via the FX desk (cash-conversion tiers, tier read off the USD amount V/1.3993); c' = same with the FX done inside the trade (UNVERIFIED reading: amounts of $60,000 or more pay the $50,000-$59,999 tier); d = US line from CAD via Norbert's gambit; e = TSX line from USD (best of FX desk and gambit). Commission C$9.99 per TSX order and US$9.99 per US order (= C$13.98 at 1.3993); the gambit's own commission is C$9.99 on each DLR.TO order and US$9.99 (default) on each DLR-U.TO order, two of each per round trip (UNRESOLVED, below). A round trip pays FX **twice** (in and out). If you never convert back (you keep USD) the FX cost is one-sided, i.e. half of the FX part.

NG half-spread sum (DLR.TO + DLR-U.TO, Norbert window 2026-07-17 to 2026-10-21): 10.1 bp. NG beats the FX desk (one way, base case, d = 0), per `cost_edge_norbert.breakeven` (USD basis): from C$1,518, and stays cheaper at every size up to C$250,000.

**Gambit assumptions.** (1) d = 0: the rate-deviation and journal-delay terms are dropped, so the gambit cost uses d = 0 (routes d and e and the break-even above). The half-spread sum used for the gambit is a high-low estimate that is biased upward (section 3), so the net direction of the error in the NG numbers is unknown: dropping d biases them down, the spread estimate biases them up. (2) **UNRESOLVED, not confirmed by the broker:** the commission on the DLR-U.TO leg. Default here: US$9.99 per order (converted at 1.3993); alternative: US$0 (if it is on the broker's $0 list). The DLR.TO leg is C$9.99. Results for both are in the table below; the verdict table (section 6) uses the default and flags any verdict that differs under the alternative.

## 5. Price-parity gap (caveat)

gap = TSX close / (US close x BoC FXUSDCAD) - 1, in bp, over the last 252 sessions per name. The BoC rate is a session average of one-minute quotes (08:00-16:00 ET), not a closing rate (same caveat as the Norbert's gambit report), and both exchanges close at 16:00 ET, so much of the dispersion is likely that averaging, not a tradable mispricing (cause UNVERIFIED).

| statistic | bp |
|---|---|
| pooled median | 1.5 |
| pooled mean | 1.5 |
| pooled p5 / p95 | -20.2 / 21.8 |
| pooled median abs | 7.9 |
| pooled p95 abs | 26.1 |

Per name median and median-abs gap are in the table in section 3. The per-name median gap is positive for 39 of 40 names (sign test p = 7.5e-11, which treats the 40 names as independent; they are not, because every name uses the same BoC rate each day); the median of the per-name medians is 1.5 bp. After removing the daily cross-name mean, the per-name median gap is positive for 23 of 40 names (p = 0.43); the common daily component accounts for 57% of the gap variance. So the one-sided sign is largely one common effect (the TSX close slightly above the US close converted at the BoC session-average rate; cause UNVERIFIED: the averaging convention, or close timing, are candidates), not many independent confirmations. The level is small and not a cost you can capture, and a close-to-close comparison cannot resolve line differences below roughly 10 bp (the pooled dispersion above).

## 7. Tax note (text only, not modelled)

**UNVERIFIED. Not reviewed by a tax adviser; do not rely on this section.** It records what was read and what was not.

- Common statement: "US-line dividends in a TFSA lose 15% to US withholding with no recovery; in an RRSP the treaty exempts it". It is stated for dividends from **US-domiciled issuers**. Whether the same withholding applies to a Canadian-incorporated issuer's dividend on its US line was not established here.
- Source rule read: dividends from a domestic (US) corporation are US-source income; dividends from a foreign corporation are US-source only if at least 25% of its gross income over the prior three years was effectively connected with a US trade or business, and then only in the pro-rata share that income bears to its total (26 U.S.C. 861(a)(2)(B), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/861, read 2026-10-23). Source is not the same as withholding: the withholding rules themselves (26 U.S.C. 871/881 and 1441/1442, the Canada-US treaty, the W-8BEN and the custody chain at the broker) were NOT read.
- **The 25% test of 861(a)(2)(B) was NOT applied** to WCN, CP, TRI or QSR (or to any other name in the list); issuers with large US operations may meet it. Treat every Canadian-incorporated name as unchecked, not as cleared.
- Where US withholding does apply (US-domiciled issuer): 15% in a TFSA, not recoverable, because the treaty does not treat a TFSA as a retirement account; RRSP/RRIF exempt, per secondary sources that cite Canada-US treaty Article XXI(2) (the article number and the non-recoverability reason are from those sources and unverified here). Sources (secondary, not the treaty text): https://www.fool.ca/2026/10/15/your-tfsa-pays-15-tax-on-u-s-dividends-your-rrsp-doesnt/, plus three broker or adviser educational pages (URLs omitted). The treaty text and a CRA / IRS publication for the non-recoverability point were not read.
- Domicile was checked for **OVV only** (hand-curated set `US_DOMICILED`). None of the flagged names is in the final list, so no name in the final list has had its domicile checked. The 40 names in the final list that are not flagged are **unchecked** (country of incorporation not looked up); this is not a finding that they are Canadian-domiciled. Partnership units (e.g. Bermuda LPs) were not considered.
- Not modelled: the cost of converting USD dividends from the US line back to CAD, and the capital-gains and superficial-loss treatment of the two lines. This is not tax advice.

## 8. Caveats

- Spreads are high-low estimates, not quotes; depth and market impact at C$100k-250k are not captured. Check live bid/ask for the less liquid names.
